# Design Reference: Mindful Editorial Productivity

## 1. Design Identity

**Style:** Mindful Editorial Productivity

Create calm, intentional, human-centered productivity interfaces that combine **editorial elegance, organic warmth, and functional clarity**.

The experience should feel grounded, refined, and approachable—not corporate, overly gamified, or visually overwhelming.

**Core principles:**
- **Calm Technology:** Minimize visual noise and cognitive load.
- **Editorial Craftsmanship:** Use expressive typography, thoughtful spacing, and deliberate hierarchy.
- **Intentional Productivity:** Emphasize sustainable routines, focus, and progress over urgency.
- **Human-Centered Collaboration:** Support individual and shared workflows without unnecessary complexity.

## 2. Visual Design System

### Colors
- **Primary:** Deep forest green `#2E5A44` for primary actions, emphasis, and identity.
- **Background:** Warm off-white `#F8FAF6` for soft, paper-like surfaces.
- **Surfaces:** Linen, stone, and organic neutral tones.
- **Accents:** Muted sage, terracotta peach, and warm amber for categories, priorities, and statuses.
- Avoid saturated colors, harsh contrasts, and excessive visual alerts.

### Typography
- **Display:** Editorial serif typography (Newsreader or Garamond-style) for headings, key metrics, and timers.
- **Interface:** Clean, modern sans-serif for body text, labels, metadata, navigation, and controls.
- Establish hierarchy through typography, spacing, and weight rather than excessive decoration.

### Layout and Components
- Soft, rounded containers with **8–16px border radii**.
- Pill-shaped badges and subtle categorical indicators.
- Circular progress rings and restrained data visualizations.
- Generous whitespace and consistent spacing.
- Minimal borders, subtle surface separation, and lightweight visual treatments.
- Clear grouping and progressive disclosure of information.

## 3. UX Architecture

### Information Hierarchy
Prioritize information in this order:

1. **Overview:** Daily rhythm, progress, focus, and relevant activity.
2. **Context:** Schedules, participants, categories, and priorities.
3. **Details:** Individual tasks, subtasks, habits, and actions.

Interfaces should be immediately scannable while allowing deeper exploration.

### Individual and Collaborative Workflows
Support both **solo productivity and lightweight collaboration** as first-class experiences.

- Individual workflows must remain simple and independent.
- Collaboration should be optional and unobtrusive.
- Use subtle participant avatars, ownership indicators, and contextual controls.
- Favor companion accountability over rigid enterprise-style assignment structures.

### Interaction Design
- Favor predictable, low-friction interactions.
- Use progressive disclosure to prevent unnecessary complexity.
- Provide clear feedback without excessive animations or notifications.
- Make primary actions obvious while keeping secondary actions visually restrained.

## 4. Content and Brand Voice

**Tone:** Calm, thoughtful, encouraging, concise, and human.

Use intentional language that reinforces sustainable productivity.

**Preferred terminology:**
- Rhythm of the Day
- Deep Rhythm
- Solo Recharge
- In Focus
- Project Stewardship

Avoid aggressive urgency, competitive gamification, and overly technical project-management language unless context requires it.

## 5. AI Implementation Guidelines

When generating or modifying interfaces:

1. Preserve the **Mindful Editorial Productivity** aesthetic consistently.
2. Favor restrained, cohesive visual compositions over decorative complexity.
3. Use forest green and warm neutrals as the dominant visual foundation.
4. Apply editorial serif typography selectively to prominent content.
5. Maintain generous spacing, soft geometry, and clear information hierarchy.
6. Present progress as meaningful feedback, not competitive pressure.
7. Keep collaborative features lightweight and optional.
8. Ensure accessible contrast, readable typography, visible focus states, and responsive layouts.
9. Reuse established design tokens and components rather than introducing inconsistent patterns.
10. Prioritize usability, clarity, and visual coherence over novelty.

## 6. Design Anti-Patterns

Avoid:
- Neon colors, excessive gradients, and heavily saturated palettes.
- Dense enterprise dashboards and visually cluttered layouts.
- Excessive shadows, borders, animations, or decorative elements.
- Aggressive notifications, gamification, and artificial urgency.
- Forcing collaboration into workflows that should remain personal.
- Sacrificing accessibility or readability for aesthetics.

## 7. Design North Star

**Build a productivity experience that feels like a thoughtfully designed journal enhanced by modern software.**

Every screen should communicate calm, clarity, purpose, and quiet sophistication while remaining functional, accessible, and efficient.
